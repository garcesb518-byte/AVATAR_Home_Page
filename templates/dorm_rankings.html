What's driving energy use across Villanova's dorms — and concrete steps every student can take to reduce it.

Energy breakdown

What's driving the bill

Across all Villanova residence halls, five categories account for nearly all energy consumption. Click any category to expand it.

Why it's so high: Heating and cooling large buildings is energy-intensive, especially in older dorms with poor insulation. Every time a window is left open while the heat or AC runs, the system works overtime to compensate.

🌡️ Keep your thermostat at a steady 68–70°F — constant cycling between extremes wastes more energy than a stable setting.
🪟 Close windows when HVAC is running. Even a small gap forces the system to run continuously.
🚪 Don't prop exterior doors open — one of the fastest ways to waste conditioned air in an entire building.
🛠️ Report malfunctioning HVAC to Facilities. A broken unit often runs non-stop, wasting energy while keeping nobody comfortable.

Why it's so high: Corridor and common area lights run 24 hours a day in most dorms. Many rooms still use older bulbs that draw 4–5× more power than LEDs for the same brightness.

💡 Switch personal lamps to LED bulbs — 75% less energy, years longer lifespan.
☀️ Use natural sunlight during the day. South-facing rooms can go hours without overhead lights on a clear day.
🌙 Start a floor habit: a nightly lights-off check of study lounges and common rooms before bed.
📢 Advocate with your RA for motion-sensor lighting in low-traffic hallways — simple upgrade, big payoff.

Why it's so high: Mini-fridges (~350 kWh/yr each), microwaves, gaming PCs, and coffee makers all add up fast. "Vampire load" from chargers on standby can account for up to 10% of a room's usage alone.

🔌 Unplug chargers and small appliances when not in use — a plugged-in charger draws power even with nothing connected.
❄️ Set your mini-fridge to 37°F — the minimum safe temperature. Every extra degree costs real energy.
🎮 Fully power down gaming consoles and monitors when leaving — sleep mode still draws significant power.
🔋 Use a smart power strip for your desk setup — it cuts power to peripherals when your main device turns off.

Why it's so high: A single 10-minute shower uses 2–5 kWh — multiplied by hundreds of residents every day, this adds up to hundreds of thousands of dollars per year across campus.

🚿 Cut your shower to 7 minutes or less. A 2-minute reduction per student could save Villanova nearly $200,000/year.
🧺 Wash laundry on cold — just as effective, cuts energy per load by up to 75%.
💧 Report dripping hot faucets to Facilities — a slow drip wastes thousands of gallons of heated water per semester.
🍽️ Air-dry dishes instead of using the heat-dry cycle on apartment-hall dishwashers.

Why it matters: Vending machines draw 2,500–3,000 kWh per year each. Shared lounges, lobby lighting, and laundry machines all contribute to base load that runs regardless of occupancy.

🛗 Take the stairs for 1–2 floors. Elevators use more energy per trip than most people realize.
📺 Turn off shared lounge TVs and lights when the space is empty.
👕 Run full laundry loads — same energy per cycle, more clothes cleaned per kWh.
🏫 Study in academic buildings during the day — that energy is spread across far more people per kWh.

Highest-impact action

One change, outsized result

Shorten your shower by 2 minutes → ~$200,000 saved per year

If every on-campus resident made this one change, Villanova could save nearly $200,000 per year — without any new technology, infrastructure, or investment. No infrastructure required. Just awareness.
